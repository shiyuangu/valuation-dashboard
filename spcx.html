How each segment is valued

Each segment reaches a steady state in horizon year T, is capitalized with a Gordon growth terminal value, and is discounted to the price-target date t0 = 2027 (MS's target is 12-month forward):

Vseg = p · FCFT · (1+g) / [ (r − g) · (1+r)T − t₀ ] ÷ N

Here p is the probability of success and N is diluted shares. Interim cash flows before T are set to zero, a simplification consistent with heavy build-out capex. HSBC, for example, sees positive free cash flow only from 2030. Net cash and debt are excluded because MS's four components sum to $299 with no separate balance-sheet line. Only Starship external launches count toward Space. Launches carrying SpaceX's own Starlink or compute payloads are costs of the other segments.

What is disclosed and what is inferred: the segment $/share values ($165, $118, $8, $8), the ~5,800 Starship launches/yr by 2040, the Cursor ARR path, and the $600 bull case are from MS as reported. Starlink Mobile is not a disclosed MS segment at all: its subscriber, price, margin and probability inputs are my own calibration (about $3/share, ≈ $36B of equity, by default; FutureSearch's third-party estimate is $75B), and MS may already include mobile inside the $118 Connectivity value, so the default total can double count it. Every other default is a calibration, not a disclosed MS input. The share count is the $1.77T IPO valuation divided by the $135 IPO price.
